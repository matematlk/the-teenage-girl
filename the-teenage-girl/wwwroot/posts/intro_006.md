Here I am in my bed, trying to go to sleep and replaying all I’ve done the past few days, as usual, when I think back 
to my last counselling appointment.

As we were going through ways to deal with periods of stress, the counsellor asked me if I had a safe space that I could 
escape to (like Sherlock Holmes in Sherlock with his mind palace). Unfortunately, that is something I’ve always thought 
about but could never quite find. So as I lay in bed, trying to go to sleep, I start attempting to build myself one, 
looking through different possible landscapes as one would look through a magazine. No image quite seems to fit however, 
and I start drifting into sleep.

My window - which is right next to my bed - is slightly open and I can feel the wind on my face. The weather is calm 
outside my house and yet I hear rain on my apartment window. Thunder cracks and I can see apartment buildings 
from my second floor view. I look across the room I am in and instantly feel calm wash over me. On the floor is a 
forever unfinished puzzle of the periodic table, strategically set on top of a puzzle mat, itself on a wooden board, 
cut especially to fit it in the case the puzzle would have to be moved. It, of course, never did move.

This puzzle is positioned right under the TV, on which the Sherlock Holmes movie staring RDJ is playing. Only it isn’t 
playing, it was paused as Sherlock was escaping a really buff villain, because my mom saw something as she was scrolling 
that messed up our 3 simultaneous screens routine.

This scene takes place during my last summer in France, during the time spent planning my move and future academic 
career. The video she saw on her phone was a metaphor involving birds and nests, referencing a child leaving. 
It would therefore come to no surprise for me to say that, as I begin to feel warmer due to the fuzzy blanket on my 
back, I also realize me and my mother are hugging on the couch, crying.

The rain is pouring twice harder now and she pulls away slightly and looks at me.
- “Je t’aime ma puce” she says.  

I could blurt out pretty much any combination of words since learning how to speak at the ripe age of 2, but these ones 
never quite came out for me. So I look at her, unable to respond, and pull her tighter, in silence.


Je t’aime maman.
